What & Why
New markets start with shallow pools (100 GIES each side), so even a single modest trade causes extreme price movement and blocks subsequent traders. This task adds a virtual liquidity layer to the AMM: at market creation the engine treats the pool as if it has extra depth, so early trades have low price impact. As real cumulative volume grows, the virtual amplification tapers off and the market transitions to fully organic price discovery.

This mirrors the bootstrapping pattern used by early Polymarket/LMSR-style AMMs — start with deep virtual liquidity, let real volume replace it over time.

Done looks like
A market freshly created at 100 GIES pool depth absorbs early trades (up to ~20 GIES) with noticeably less price movement than before
Price impact and the trade quote UI reflect the effective (amplified) depth, not just the raw pool size
As cumulative volume crosses a configurable threshold (default 1 000 GIES traded), virtual liquidity decays to zero and the market behaves identically to the current engine
Teachers can set virtualLiquidity (starting virtual depth, default 400 GIES) and virtualLiquidityDecayVolume (volume at which it reaches zero, default 1 000 GIES) when creating a market; sensible defaults apply if omitted
Existing markets without these fields continue to work unchanged (defaults of 0 virtual liquidity preserve current behaviour)
The market detail page shows a small indicator (e.g. "Bootstrapping liquidity") while virtual liquidity is still active, so students understand why early trades move the price less
Out of scope
Switching to a CLOB or order-book model
Paying real GIES tokens as liquidity incentives
Per-trade virtual liquidity adjustments (decay is based on cumulative market volume only, not per-trade)
Steps
Schema — Add virtualLiquidity (numeric, default 0) and virtualLiquidityDecayVolume (numeric, default 1 000) columns to the markets table and generate a migration.

AMM math — In amm.ts, introduce a computeEffectivePools helper that takes real pool sizes, cumulative volume, and the two new parameters, and returns amplified pool values. Wire this into quoteBuy, quoteSell, and evaluateSafeguards so all price-impact and safeguard calculations use the effective (amplified) depth. Real token balances never change — only the numbers fed into the pricing math.

Trade execution — Ensure actual token transfers and pool balance updates still operate on real pools only. The virtual layer is pricing-only and must not affect how tokens are credited or debited.

Market creation API — Accept virtualLiquidity and virtualLiquidityDecayVolume in the create-market endpoint and persist them. Apply the agreed defaults when omitted.

Frontend — market detail — Show a subtle "Bootstrapping" badge or tooltip on the probability chart / trade panel while effectiveVirtualLiquidity > 0, so students know the market is in its early phase.

Frontend — market creation form — Expose the two new fields as optional advanced settings for teachers, with inline explanations of what each controls.
